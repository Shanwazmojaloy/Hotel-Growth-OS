# Outreach templates

**Use these as scaffolding, not scripts.** An owner can tell when a message was sent to twenty people. Every template below has a bracketed `[specific]` slot — if you cannot fill it with something true about that hotel, do not send the message.

**Never** lead with: your company, "AI-powered", "we help hotels grow", a brochure, or a price list.

---

## 1. First touch — WhatsApp (after a referred introduction)

> Assalamu alaikum [name] bhai. [Referrer] suggested I talk to you — I work with hotels in [city] on keeping more of their room revenue, specifically reducing what goes to Booking.com.
>
> Two quick questions if you have a minute:
> 1. Roughly what share of your bookings come through OTAs?
> 2. How many bookings arrive by phone or at the desk?
>
> No pitch — those two numbers tell me whether I can be useful to you at all.

**Why it works:** the two questions are the feasibility inputs (they also disqualify you early if the hotel is unsuitable), and "whether I can be useful to you at all" inverts the usual vendor posture.

---

## 2. First touch — WhatsApp (cold, no introduction)

> Assalamu alaikum. I work with [city] hotels on direct bookings — the commission going to Booking.com rather than to you.
>
> [Specific observation: "I noticed you're listed on Booking.com at ৳8,500 but your own site shows ৳7,200 — that gap is a commission conversation."]
>
> Two questions: what share of your bookings are OTA, and how many come by phone?
>
> If the numbers say I can't help, I'll say so and stop taking your time.

**The `[specific observation]` slot is mandatory on cold outreach.** It is the only thing that distinguishes the message from the twenty other vendor messages the owner ignores each week. Spend ten minutes verifying the observation before sending.

---

## 3. Same-day follow-up after a call

> Thanks for the time, [name] bhai. As discussed — send me three months of your booking report and one month of the Booking.com statements, and I'll come back with a one-page read on what's actually available. No charge for that, and no obligation.
>
> If the numbers say it isn't worth your money, that's what the page will say.

---

## 4. The data-share ask (rung 3 — the most important message in the kit)

> For the read, the three things I need are:
>
> 1. Monthly booking report for the last 3 months — room nights, ADR, and source if it's in there (an Excel export is fine, a photo of a printout works)
> 2. One Booking.com statement (recent month) — I want the commission lines, not the totals
> 3. Your website's booking confirmation (just a screenshot of what a guest sees)
>
> Everything stays with me, nothing goes to a third party, and I'll delete it if we don't proceed. I can sign an NDA if that's easier.

**Why item 3:** it lets you check whether conversion tracking is even working — frequently it isn't, and finding that out is the single most credible thing you can tell an owner in the first week.

---

## 5. Delivering the one-page read

> [name] bhai — here's the read, one page, from your own statements.
>
> The headline: roughly ৳[X] a month is going to platforms for guests who had already chosen you — past guests, repeat corporate, people who searched your name. [If true: "Also, your website's confirmation page isn't recording conversions properly, which is worth fixing whether or not we work together."]
>
> What's capturable conservatively is ৳[Y]/month — I'm quoting the low end on purpose.
>
> If that looks wrong to you, tell me and I'll check my working. If it looks right, I'd like to walk you through the 90 days.

**Rule: if the read says "not viable", send it anyway and say so.** One honest no costs you a bad client and buys the referral that follows.

---

## 6. Soft close (no pressure, real deadline)

> I'm taking on three design partners this quarter and I have [N] slots. The arrangement is 90 days, ৳[fee]/month, you keep ownership of every account and set the spend ceiling, and you can cancel with 14 days' notice.
>
> Would you rather I put the terms in writing, or would you like to see the first month's measurement plan first?

Giving two acceptable next steps is more effective than one — and both are small.

---

## 7. Re-engagement (day 10 — value, no ask)

> [name] bhai, unrelated to anything — [one genuinely useful observation from public data: "your Booking.com listing ranks above two comparable properties in [area] for 'hotel in [area]', worth knowing for your rate decisions."]
>
> No action needed. Just thought you'd want to see it.

---

## 8. Honest close-out (day 21)

> I'll stop chasing, [name] bhai. If the season changes how this looks, or if the OTA commission starts to bite, message me and we'll pick it up.
>
> Either way — the one-page read is yours to keep.

**This message produces more future replies than any follow-up.** It is also the truth.

---

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Because |
|---|---|
| Send a proposal or deck before a call | Proposals get read as "vendor", and the read/generated one-pager is strictly better |
| Follow up without adding new information | "Just following up" teaches the owner to ignore you |
| Quote a commission figure you didn't compute | The entire pitch is arithmetic; one invented number ends it |
| Chase a hotel you know cannot work | Costs the scarcest resource — a call — and ends in churn |
| Offer a discount to close | Prices are the *design-partner* rate already; discounting signals the value was never real |
| Promise a booking count | See playbook objection 6 |
