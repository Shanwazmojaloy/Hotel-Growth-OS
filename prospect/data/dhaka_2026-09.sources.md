# Dhaka target list — provenance and limits

Compiled 16 September 2026 from public sources only. **No figure in `dhaka_2026-09.csv` was invented.** Every row carries source IDs, and every field that is an assumption rather than a measurement is named in its `assumed_fields` column.

This file exists because a prospect list is a data product, and this one is going to a stranger who will check it. A list that overstates what is known gets caught on the first call.

---

## 1. Why Dhaka, and why not Cox's Bazar yet

**Dhaka** has year-round business demand: corporate travel, embassies, garment-sector procurement, medical tourism, and a steady airport corridor. Bookings arrive by phone and at the front desk — which is the volume the economics depend on.

**Cox's Bazar is still the second list**, and for a specific reason rather than a general one: a Cox's Bazar hotel that runs at 95% for three months and 25% for the rest would screen as viable on its annual average while the off-season months fail the break-even test outright — and **no public source states a hotel's monthly occupancy**, so the ratio has to come from the hotel rather than from research.

The screener now carries a one-way seasonal downgrade (`season_ratio`, threshold 1.6): declare a ratio and the weakest months are tested against break-even, and a verdict can only ever move *down*. That is a guard, not a model — it stops a peak-season hotel being sold as a year-round one, and it cannot tell you which months are weak or what a season-weighted fee should be. The full monthly model lives in `measurement/seasonality.py` and needs the property's own PMS history, which is exactly what a signed design partner provides. Practical consequence: Cox's Bazar properties belong in the funnel, but they are screened *with* the ratio the owner states, and the offer is season-weighted from the first conversation.

## 3. What is measured, what is assumed

| Field | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| `rooms` | **Measured** | Published by a source (§2). Where two sources disagree, the row is marked `rooms_status=disputed` and the conflict is named in the note. |
| `adr_bdt` | **Partly measured** | Set from a live 2026 listing where one exists (`adr_basis=listed`). Where no live rate was captured, the star-class average from S6 is used and the row is marked `adr_basis=class_avg`. |
| `occupancy` | **Assumed** | 0.52 for every row — the national average recorded in `01-adjudication.md`. Per-hotel occupancy is not public anywhere. |
| `ota_share` | **Assumed** | By segment: 0.45 (5★ independent), 0.50 (4★), 0.60 (3★ city), 0.65 (budget/airport). Informed by the pack's OTA benchmarks; **not** measured per property. |
| `effective_ota_commission` | **Assumed** | 0.18 (5★), 0.17 (4★), 0.16 (3★/budget) — Booking's standard 15% plus Genius/visibility loading, below the 18–22% Expedia range in `01-adjudication.md`. |
| `direct_share` | **Deliberately blank** | No public source publishes a hotel's direct share. This is the one field that has to come from the owner's mouth, which is exactly why the opener asks for it. |

### Why the rate figures are conservative, not flattering

A "from $X" listing price is the **cheapest bookable room**, not ADR — the true average across room types is higher. This biases the screen in the safe direction on both outputs:

- **The required lift gets harder** (a lower ADR means the fee buys back fewer room-nights), so nothing passes on an inflated rate.
- **The prize gets smaller** (commission and capturable are computed from that rate), so the screen cannot promise more than the arithmetic supports.

Every rate here should be read as a floor. A hotel that clears break-even on its floor rate clears it on its real ADR too — which is the only direction of error worth having in a list you are going to read out loud.

## 4. Coverage and the honest gap

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Independent hotels in Dhaka per S1 | **148** |
| Properties identified from public sources | 56 |
| **Screenable** (room count published) | **18** — `dhaka_2026-09.csv` |
| Watchlist (live rate known, room count not published) | 40 — `dhaka_watchlist.csv` |
| Deliberately excluded (international chains) | 9 — §5 |

**The gap is the finding.** Room counts are published for barely a third of the properties found, and the screener will not rank on an invented one. Public data alone yields **18 screenable hotels in Dhaka**, not the 40 the funnel in `sales-kit/first-45-days-activity.md` assumes.

Three ways to close it, in the order I would try them:

1. **Verify the watchlist by phone or Google Maps listing** (40 properties, ~2 minutes each). Most independent Dhaka hotels answer the phone and will state their room count. This alone takes the screenable list to roughly 50.
2. **Widen to Chattogram**, which has a comparable business-hotel stock and better year-round corporate demand than Cox's Bazar.
3. **Accept a smaller first cohort.** Eighteen screened properties at a 50% call rate is nine conversations — enough for three design partners if the conversion holds, and it is honest about what public data can support.

Which of these is worth the hours is a judgement call, but understating the list's size is not: the funnel's 40 does not exist yet in Dhaka public data, and a plan that assumes it does will show up as a week-6 shortfall.

**Rule applied:** directory prices refresh slowly and Booking/KAYAK rates are live, so live rates win in `dhaka_2026-09.csv`. Where only a directory price exists and the class average is available, the class average is used and flagged.

## 7. Refresh discipline

This list has a shelf life of roughly **one month**. Listing prices move, hotels change flags, and two airports-corridor properties are mid-renovation.

To refresh: re-pull S6 (live rates), re-check the disputed rows, and re-run

```bash
python3 -m prospect.cli screen --input prospect/data/dhaka_2026-09.csv --fee 19000 --media 50000 --out prospect/reports
```

The verdicts change with the inputs; nothing in the call sheet should be quoted from a figure older than the last refresh.

## 8. What this list does not contain

- **No phone numbers or emails.** Contact details were not captured, and inventing a plausible-looking number is worse than leaving the field out. Each prospect needs one Google Maps lookup before a call — 30 seconds, and it also confirms the property still trades under that name.
- **No owner or manager names.** Public sources do not carry them reliably; the front desk knows who to transfer to.
- **No seasonality.** See §1.
- **No OTA-dependence measurement.** Review counts (S7) are a proxy that ranks properties by how much of their business flows through OTA platforms, and nothing more than that.

---

## What this file does not contain

**No `season_ratio` column.** Every row therefore reads as flat, and the screener's seasonal
downgrade is inert on this list. That is a decision about Dhaka business hotels (year-round
demand, no owner-stated peak/trough ratio available from public sources), not an observation —
and it is the first thing to change for a leisure market, where the ratio has to come from the
owner and cannot be inferred from public data.
